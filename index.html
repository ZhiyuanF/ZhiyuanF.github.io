Zhiyuan Fan

Postdoctoral Fellow in Power System Economics

Harvard John A. Paulson School of Engineering and Applied Sciences (SEAS)
Salata Institute for Climate and Sustainability

I study how incentives, markets, and strategic behavior shape long-term energy transitions. My research combines energy systems, game theory, market design, and economics, with a growing focus on how agentic AI changes economic decision-making and market behavior.

Mentors and collaborators

At Harvard, I work with Prof. Le Xie and Prof. James H. Stock through the Harvard John A. Paulson School of Engineering and Applied Sciences and the Salata Institute for Climate and Sustainability. I completed my Ph.D. in Earth and Environmental Engineering at Columbia University with Prof. Bolun Xu, and previously worked with Dr. S. Julio Friedmann at Columbia's Center on Global Energy Policy. I am also fortunate to collaborate with Prof. Tianyi Lin on game theory, market design, and energy economics.

Research

Energy infrastructure and long-term transition

Investment, deployment, and transformation of energy systems under decarbonization.

Incentive and market design

Mechanisms for long-term investment, reliability, flexibility, and climate mitigation.

Game theory and strategic decision-making

Strategic interactions among firms, nations, and other actors in energy systems.

Energy economics and policy

Economic drivers, policy interventions, and decisions under uncertainty in the energy transition.

Agentic AI for economic decision-making

How AI agents reshape strategic behavior, bidding, markets, and mechanism design.
