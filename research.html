Research

Research

My primary research interest is incentive design for long-term energy transition. I study how infrastructure investment and climate mitigation emerge from decentralized decisions, and how markets and policy can better align those decisions with system-level objectives.

Research agenda

Energy infrastructure and long-term transition

I study investment, deployment, and transformation of energy infrastructure under decarbonization. The emphasis is on long-horizon decisions—what gets built, where investment occurs, which technologies diffuse, and how strategic behavior changes transition pathways.

Incentive and market design

A central theme of my work is designing incentives that support socially valuable investment and behavior. Applications include renewable programs, reliability and flexibility, carbon management, and emerging low-carbon infrastructure.

Game theory and strategic decision-making

I use game-theoretic models to study interacting decisions by firms, utilities, nations, and other strategic actors. This perspective helps connect local incentives to system-wide and international climate outcomes.

Energy economics and policy

My work examines how economic conditions, policy interventions, market structure, uncertainty, and energy security shape investment and technology adoption during the energy transition.

Agentic AI for economic decision-making

I am increasingly interested in economic environments in which AI agents are decision-makers rather than merely predictive tools. Current work examines bidding agents in electricity markets and the implications of agentic AI for strategic behavior, market outcomes, and mechanism design.
